## Coordinates

6.45407, 3.39467 (latitude, longitude, WGS 84)

## Administrative context

Lagos State is one of Nigeria's 36 states, and its capital is Ikeja. The state is divided into 20 local government areas (LGAs), which are listed in the Constitution.

Source: [Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999](https://nigeriareposit.nln.gov.ng/server/api/core/bitstreams/01016815-6c76-4370-a9c4-0369458e9312/content) (National Library of Nigeria repository), s. 3(1), 3(3) and 3(6) (p. 16), and First Schedule, Part I, Lagos row (p. 125).

The World Bank also reported 37 Local Council Development Areas (LCDAs) in 2010, alongside the 20 LGAs; the current number has not been confirmed here.

Source: [World Bank, *Nigeria: Lagos Rolling Public Expenditure Review 1*, May 2010](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/792901468099260455/pdf/679360ESW0P1170IC0disclosed04050120.pdf) (document 67936), p. 12.

There is no official boundary for "the city of Lagos" in the sources used here. The World Bank describes Lagos State as "largely a city state", with Lagos Island, Victoria Island and Ikoyi forming the "heartland of the City of Lagos", a mainland area that includes Ikeja, Surulere, Yaba and Ebute-Meta, and outskirts at Ikorodu, Epe and Badagry. Neither source says which LGAs make up the city. This matches OpenStreetMap, which maps the city only as a point (see Identifiers).

Source: [World Bank, May 2010](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/792901468099260455/pdf/679360ESW0P1170IC0disclosed04050120.pdf), p. 12.

Lagos was Nigeria's federal capital until 12 December 1991, when the seat of government formally moved to Abuja. Lagos Island had served as both the federal capital and the state capital; the state capital moved to Ikeja after the Federal Capital Territory was created in 1976. The Constitution now names the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja as the capital of the Federation.

Sources: [World Bank, May 2010](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/792901468099260455/pdf/679360ESW0P1170IC0disclosed04050120.pdf), p. 12; [Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999](https://nigeriareposit.nln.gov.ng/server/api/core/bitstreams/01016815-6c76-4370-a9c4-0369458e9312/content), s. 298 (p. 113).

## Competing definitions

Lagos has no official city boundary, so the population depends on which area you count. These figures use different areas, years and methods, so they aren't directly comparable.

| Definition | Population | Year | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lagos State (20 LGAs), census | 9,113,605 | 2006 | [Official Gazette No. 2, Vol. 96, 2 Feb 2009](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ng/2009/ng-government-gazette-dated-2009-02-02-no-2.pdf), pp. B 34–B 35 |
| Lagos urban agglomeration (built-up area), UN estimate | 13,463,000 | 2018 | [UN, *World Urbanization Prospects 2018: Highlights*](https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/files/documents/2020/Feb/un_2018_wup_highlights.pdf), Table II, p. 29 |
| Lagos State, state government figure | "22mn+" | Not given | [Lagos State Government](https://lagosstate.gov.ng/), viewed 21 Oct 2026 |
| GeoNames (area not defined) | 15,388,000 | Not given | [GeoNames 2332459](https://www.geonames.org/2332459/lagos.html) |

The census is the only official count, but it's from 2006. The UN figure covers only the built-up area yet is higher, probably partly because it's 12 years later and is an estimate rather than a count; no source here reconciles the two. The state government and GeoNames figures give no year or method, so they can't be checked.

## Coverage notes

- **No city boundary.** Datasets may use the city point (OSM node 27565124, GeoNames 2332459) or the whole of Lagos State (OSM relation 3718182). Results will differ a lot depending on which one is used.
- **City and state get mixed up.** Version 26 of OSM relation 3718182 removed a `place=city` tag from the state boundary, which shows the two being confused in the data itself ([relation history](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/3718182/history)).
- **Population figures are old or unverifiable.** The only official count is the 2006 census; other figures are estimates or have no year or method (see Competing definitions).
- **Sub-state units may have changed.** The 20 LGAs are fixed in the Constitution, but the LCDA count (37 in 2010) may have changed, so any data split by LCDA should be checked against a current list.
- **Not researched:** air-quality monitoring stations and informal settlement areas. These should be checked before using Lagos in coverage-sensitive analysis.
